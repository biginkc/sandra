# Blind offer percentage: deep-research report (2026-11-02)

**Question:** what percentage of the DealMachine estimated value should the printed, sight-unseen cash offer be?

**Short answer:** nobody publishes it. No source found reveals the Rapid Offer System meter percentages, and no source reports reply, appointment, or contract rates for a priced blind offer on houses in a Midwest market. The only published formulas are vendor-side ("approximate ARV x a value percentage you pick") and the only hard numbers are from academic negotiation studies, which say: a single number anchors harder than a range, a precise-to-the-dollar number anchors harder but deters people from replying at all, and ranges anchor on both ends. The percentage has to come from your own closed deals and a split test.

## How to read this report

- Run: 100 agents, 18 sources fetched, 78 claims extracted, 25 verified by three independent votes each, 14 confirmed, 11 "refuted".
- **"Refuted" mostly means "could not re-fetch the page"**, not "false". Columbia, StreetInsider, and Scinapse returned 403 or a CAPTCHA to the verifiers; BiggerPockets and Clever claims were voted down because the verifier could not see the page. Where the original fetch agent quoted the page verbatim, I keep the quote and mark the row "quoted, not re-verified".
- The 2026-11-01 note in this folder (`2026-11-01-rapid-offer-percentage.md`) holds the Flip With Rick video notes. Those came from a prior session's reading of the video, not from this harness, and are marked as such below.

## 2. Best estimate of the Rapid Offer System meter percentages

**No source states them. Any specific number here would be invented.** What the evidence supports:

- The base is an approximate ARV, not an AVM (row 2, low confidence).
- Meter 1 is lowest, 4 highest; Flip With Rick recommend 2 for Midwest markets and 3 for competitive markets (rows 1 and 4).
- Their own story (row 4): the first mailer at roughly 50-60% of value got no replies; a much higher mailer flooded them with tire kickers; the middle setting worked. That brackets the working settings somewhere between "above 60%" and "below retail", which is consistent with the 65-75% candidate range a practitioner floated in 2024 (row 10) and the 70-75% that real cash buyers pay (row 13).

Confidence that meter 2-3 lands in the 65-80% of ARV band: low to medium. Confidence in any specific number per setting: none. The one way to get it is in row 1: OLM shows you the first 10 generated offers at each setting. Run the same 10 addresses through DealMachine, divide, and you have the percentages in an afternoon.

## 3. Recommendation for a Kansas City blind offer

⭐ **Single number, 75% of the DealMachine estimated value, rounded to the nearest $1,000, flat across price bands to start.** Why:

- 75% sits inside the only working range anyone describes (rows 4, 10, 13) and matches the ceiling Sandra already enforces (>80% ask = "price too high").
- Not to the dollar. The ROS card prints $185,239.00. Peer-reviewed evidence (rows 20-21) says precision to that degree makes people less willing to start talking, and your first goal is the reply. Round to $1,000 ($139,000), not to $5,000 or $10,000, so it still reads as computed rather than tossed off.
- Single number, not a range. The one range type that beats a single number (row 23) puts your real figure at the top and extends LOWER, which cuts the headline number and the reply rate. Any range that extends upward is a backdown range and did worse than a point offer, and practitioners report sellers hear only the top (row 8).
- Keep DealMachine's value as the base even though ROS uses ARV. Row 18 says off-market AVM error is roughly 7-11% median in the Midwest with no known direction, so a 75% print of a typical-condition value will land near 65-70% of true ARV on dated houses, which is where real buyers transact.

**Tiers, if you want them later.** No source supports tiering by price band. The prior note's tier table (50-60% under $100k rising to 72-75% above $350k) is reasoning from fixed costs, not data. Start flat and let section 4 tell you whether bands diverge.

**Condition clause.** The only field-tested wording is Flip With Rick's "property condition review" (their claim that it beat "inspection", row 4, unverified) and the ROS card's "*Offer is Subject to Property Condition Review" (row 3). Row 24 says sellers accept a cut only for things they did not already know about, so the clause should name condition, not inspection or financing. Suggested postcard/text line, for your approval:

```
Cash offer: $139,000, as-is. Offer is subject to property condition review.
```

If you want one more sentence to soften the retrade: "If the house needs more work than typical for the area, the number will change. If it is in average shape, this is the number."

## 4. Three biggest uncertainties, and how your own data resolves them

1. **Where does DealMachine's value sit against true ARV and against what you actually pay, by band?** For every closed and dead deal, record DealMachine value at offer time, your walkthrough ARV, contract price, and resale or assignment price. Compute contract / DealMachine value and DealMachine value / ARV. Median and spread by band answers whether 75% is right and whether bands need different numbers. Twenty deals is enough to see the shape.

2. **How far does the final price move from the printed number, and does the move kill deals?** Track printed offer, first verbal offer, contract price, and whether the seller cancelled or complained. Retrade size and cancel rate by opening percentage tells you whether 75% is too high (big cuts, angry sellers) or too low (replies accept as printed, you leave margin).

3. **Does the number or its format change the reply rate at all?** Nothing in the research is a sight-unseen offer to a seller; it is MBA students and Redfin listings. Split the same list at random: 70% vs 75% vs 80%, and rounded vs to-the-dollar. Track reply, appointment, contract. Since Sandra already classifies replies, the reply-quality split (motivated vs "take me off" vs angry) comes for free.
