# Rapid Offer: what % of the DealMachine value to text (research, 2026-11-01)

## Bottom line
Two different numbers are in play. Decide which one Rapid Offer sends.
- **Engagement number** (Flip With Rick "Rapid Offer System"): a marketing number meant to get the reply, renegotiated after a "property condition review". Their data: 50-60% = crickets, ~90% = flooded with tire kickers. Sweet spot implied ~70-80%. They refuse to publish the exact %.
- **Real buy number** (70% rule and KC buyer threads): 50-75% of value minus repairs, rising with price.
Recommended start: **75% of DealMachine estimated value, flat, "subject to property condition review"**, then calibrate from closed deals. Sandra already treats >80% ask as "price too high", so 75-80 is the ceiling you've set yourself.

## What DealMachine's number is
- API field `estimated_value` = "Estimated market value" (county records + nearby sales). No stated condition, no vendor named. No separate ARV field.
- DealMachine's own wholesaling calculator pre-fills ARV with this estimate.
- Judgment: typical-condition value. Above as-is for dated houses, below true ARV for a fresh rehab. Confidence ~65%.
- Marketing claim "±3% ARV accuracy" on the Comps add-on, unverified. No KC / distressed accuracy data found.
Sources: api.docs.dealmachine.com get-property; help.next.dealmachine.com wholesaling-calculator; next.dealmachine.com/features/comps-analysis.

## Video notes (Flip With Rick / Zach Gin, "How to Give Cash Offers on Your Direct Mail")
- Started from the two most common seller replies: "take me off your list" and "you're a cash buyer, what's your offer?"
- Mailer 1 at ~50-60 cents: bomb. Mailer 2 much higher: phone rang off hook, all tire kickers. Mailer 3: middle, fewer calls, real leads.
- "It doesn't matter what you offer. The point is to get the prospect to engage you." Then standard qualification; they rarely pay the printed number ($100k offer -> $40k contract is their pitch).
- Fine print tested: "property condition review" beat "inspection" and "closing" wording.
- Offer meter 1-4: 1 lowball, 2 easy/Midwest market, 3 competitive market, 4 creative/aggressive. They recommend 2-3.
- Response rates: 2.4-2.5% their market (FL), 3.87% Midwest test. Follow-up mailers lower the offer to create FOMO.
- They tease an SMS version of the same offers (Batch, Launch Control, Lead Sherpa).

## Formula research
- 70% rule = ARV x 70% - repairs - fee. 30% held back ~15% fixed costs + 15% profit. Cash buyers stretch to 75-80%; hard-money 65-75%.
- Cheap houses break the rule (fixed costs don't shrink). KC buyer threads (2021, thin): 70% minus repairs standard, 80% in hot pockets called risky.
- Blind repair allowance: ~$30-40/sq ft medium in KC, kept as a separate dollar amount. 2026 guide: light $25-40, moderate $50-75, gut $90-135+.
- HomeVestors-type blind buyers: ~50-70% of ARV minus repairs (promotional sources). iBuyers ~98.6% of AVM but only clean homes; not comparable.
- KC flip rate 3rd-4th highest among large metros (ATTOM Q1 2025/2026): lots of competing cash.
- Retrade risk: sellers read a post-walkthrough price cut as bait-and-switch; no hard data on response rate by offer level.
- Range offers: bottom = your real number; precise figures hold firmer than round ones (Ames & Mason 2015, unverified from memory).

## Suggested tiers for a real-buy number (if you want one instead of a flat engagement %)
| Value band | % of estimate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| <$100k | 50-60% | fixed costs + $25-30k min profit dominate; least reliable estimates |
| $100-200k | 62-68% | core KC flip range |
| $200-350k | 68-72% | competing with 75-80% buyers |
| $350k+ | 72-75% | few distressed sellers here |
